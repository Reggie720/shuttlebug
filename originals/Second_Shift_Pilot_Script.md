# SECOND SHIFT
## Episode 1: The First Customer
Original short drama for Shuttlebug / A Hog Entertainment. Written November 26, 2026.

**Status:** complete shooting-script draft; not filmed. Target length: 3–4 minutes, subject to rehearsal. Fictional people and business. No existing show, celebrity likeness, film footage or third-party music is required.

**Format:** natural live-action performance. One kitchen table after dark. Two adult performers, MARCUS and his sister TESS. A phone, a notebook, an empty envelope, two mugs. No moving vehicle, special effects or licensed location is needed. Record the actors' own voices. Use room sound; no synthetic narration or unlicensed background music.

### 1. THE TABLE — NIGHT (about 45 seconds)
Wide, steady shot. MARCUS has written SECOND SHIFT across a notebook. Beneath it: CUSTOMERS. The rest of the page is empty.

TESS sets down two mugs.

TESS: You been staring at that phone so long, it's gonna charge you rent.

MARCUS: Waiting on an answer.

TESS: From who?

MARCUS: Anybody.

She turns the notebook toward herself.

TESS: That's a lot of people doing nothing.

MARCUS: I sent the messages. Made the page. Set everything up.

TESS: What are you selling?

MARCUS: Deliveries. Small stuff. After work.

TESS: Then say that. You wrote a whole speech about a company. People need somebody to pick up a box.

### 2. THE OFFER (about 60 seconds)
Medium shot, both at the table. Marcus crosses out three lines on a loose sheet.

MARCUS: "Evening pickup. One small package. Tell me where it's going."

TESS: Better.

MARCUS: Sounds too small.

TESS: It is small. That's why you can do it tonight.

He begins typing. Stops.

MARCUS: What if they ask how many customers I got?

TESS: Tell them the truth.

MARCUS: Zero isn't much of an advertisement.

TESS: Neither is a promise you can't keep.

Silence. Marcus types again.

MARCUS: "I'm starting with one route so I can do it right."

TESS: There you go.

He taps SEND. Set the phone face down. Do not photograph real messages or customer information.

### 3. THE WAIT (about 45 seconds)
Close on the notebook. Marcus draws a box beside CUSTOMERS.

MARCUS: Now we wait.

TESS: No. Now you figure out what happens if somebody says yes.

MARCUS: I pick it up.

TESS: Where do you write the address? Who confirms it got there? What if you're late?

He pulls the notebook closer.

MARCUS: You been saving all these questions?

TESS: Since the first time you said "I've got a plan."

They share a small laugh. He writes three headings: PICKUP. CONTACT. CONFIRMATION.

### 4. THE FIRST YES (about 45 seconds)
The phone vibrates. Make the sound on set or record an original phone vibration. No branded notification tune.

Marcus looks. Then puts the phone down carefully.

TESS: Bad news?

MARCUS: Worse. They said yes.

TESS: Good. Read it.

MARCUS: "Can you pick up one box tomorrow evening?"

TESS: Can you?

He checks his notebook. A beat.

MARCUS: Yes. If we agree on the details first.

TESS: Look at you. A whole company.

He checks the first box. Cut to black before any fake order or income is shown.

**END CARD:** SECOND SHIFT — A Shuttlebug original production concept.
